Context

APEX STUDENT is an AI scheduling app for students. I came in when it was a product with no brand: no site, no visual language, no personality. This is the project where I owned the whole surface, everything a user sees before they trust the app enough to hand it their calendar.

The problem

Productivity tools for students face a paradox: students need structure and resent being told to have it. A scheduling app that markets like a productivity guru gets ignored; one that's too playful doesn't get trusted with your semester. Apex needed to feel like relief, not homework.

The thinking

The bet: give the AI a personality instead of an interface. Scheduling is stressful. A system is cold, but a character is company.

That's LOLA. Not a decoration, a strategic decision. LOLA lets Apex say things a SaaS brand can't ("you scheduled 4 hours of 'grind time' and opened TikTok, be serious"). She makes the AI feel like a companion instead of surveillance, and she gives every surface a consistent narrator, from onboarding to empty states to social.

The work

Brand system

Logo, color, type, and voice, built around one idea: calm structure, warm voice. And a face to carry it, LOLA, the study companion at the center of the whole system.

Character direction: personality spec, tone rules, and a full expression set, so LOLA can react across the product with a consistent voice. And a note on where LOLA deliberately doesn't appear. Nobody wants a mascot in a billing screen or an error state.

Product UI

The app itself, designed end-to-end: today view, calendar, the LOLA chat, the efficiency map, the weekly plan, and the menu. A narrative that moves from problem to relief to proof, kept consistent from marketing into the product.

Social content

A content system where LOLA carries the feed: a consistent card format that turns each app value, clarity, balance, performance, into a shareable slide.

Onboarding

A first-run experience designed as a moment with LOLA rather than a form. The brand promise kept inside the product, from the loading screen on.

Killed Direction

We started as a very generic calendar app, the kind no one would actually use. We killed it. What we're building now is encouragement and directed motivation: an app that helps you maximize your day and stop scrolling, not just another grid to ignore.

Where it stands

APEX is still pre-launch: no downloads yet, because there's nothing to download yet. But the brand is already working. The complete system, site, character, and content engine, is built, and LOLA-led content consistently outperforms everything without her.

Brand system
Logo, mark, palette, type, key visual and the rules that hold them together — plus LOLA, drawn as a full character set rather than a single mascot pose.
Surfaces
The website, the blog and newsletter, onboarding, in-app illustration and the social content engine.
What we learned
Content with LOLA in it consistently outperforms content without her. That is the whole argument for building a character instead of a logo.
Status
Still pre-launch — there is nothing to download yet, so there are no download numbers to quote. The brand is finished and waiting on the build.
What I'd do next

Push LOLA into retention: streak moments, semester-recap content ("LOLA wrapped"), and a TikTok presence where the character posts, not the company.
